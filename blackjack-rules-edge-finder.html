How to read this tool

3:2 matters most

Changing blackjack payout from 3:2 to 6:5 is one of the largest common rule downgrades.

Soft 17 matters

Dealer hitting soft 17 usually increases the house edge compared with standing on soft 17.

Bankroll impact is volume

A small edge can still cost meaningful money when average bet size and hands per hour are high.

How the estimate is built

The tool starts from a common 6-deck baseline and adds a fixed adjustment for each rule that differs from it. These are basic-strategy estimates, not exact combinatorial results.

The formulas

  • House edge = 0.40% + the sum of the rule adjustments
  • Expected loss per hour = bet × hands per hour × house edge
  • Session expected loss = bet × hands per hour × hours × house edge
  • Expected loss vs bankroll = session expected loss ÷ session bankroll
  • Typical swing ≈ 1.15 × bet × √(hands per hour × hours), with flat bets (about 1.05 instead of 1.15 when doubling is not allowed)

Worked example

At $25 per hand, 60 hands per hour for 2 hours, total action is $3,000. On the 3:2, S17 baseline (0.40%) the expected session loss is $25 × 60 × 2 × 0.40% = $12. The same game paying 6:5 (1.80%) costs $25 × 60 × 2 × 1.80% = $54.

Swings dominate short sessions. With a per-hand standard deviation of about 1.15 units, one standard deviation over those 120 hands is about 1.15 × $25 × √120 ≈ $315, far more than the $12 expected loss.
