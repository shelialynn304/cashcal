How We Review Casinos

Our review process starts with the parts players can verify: rules, payment language, RTP disclosures, licensing claims, responsible gambling controls, and bonus terms. A flashy promotion does not cancel out unclear rules or high bankroll pressure.

  • RTP transparency: Whether slots and digital games publish meaningful return-to-player information.
  • House edge clarity: Whether common bets and game versions make the casino advantage easy to understand.
  • Blackjack and roulette rule quality: Payouts, deck rules, S17/H17 details, wheel type, and other rules that change expected value.
  • Withdrawal and payment clarity: Deposit options, withdrawal steps, processing expectations, fees, and identity checks.
  • Responsible gambling tools: Deposit limits, cooling-off periods, self-exclusion access, reality checks, and help resources.
  • State availability / licensing clarity: Clear information about where the casino operates and which rules apply.
  • Bonus terms clarity: Wagering requirements, game contribution rules, expiration windows, caps, and withdrawal restrictions.

Casino Review FAQ

Do casino reviews require affiliate links?

No. A casino review can be useful without affiliate links. Edge Over Luck may add affiliate links in the future, but the review standard should still focus on rules, transparency, risk, and responsible gambling information.

What makes a casino mathematically better or worse?

Better rules, clearer RTP disclosures, fairer blackjack payouts, lower-house-edge game options, and plain-language terms can improve the player side of the math. None of that makes gambling profitable or safe.

Why does house edge matter?

House edge is the casino’s long-run mathematical advantage. It does not predict one spin, hand, or session, but it explains expected value and why short-term wins do not erase long-term risk.

Are bonuses always good?

No. A bonus can look generous while hiding high wagering requirements, game restrictions, withdrawal caps, short deadlines, or other terms that make it less useful than it appears.

Can casino math make gambling safe?

No. Casino math can help you understand risk, variance, expected value, and bankroll pressure. It cannot remove the house edge, prevent losses, or make gambling risk-free.
