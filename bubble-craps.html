How Bubble Craps Works

Bubble craps is easier to learn when you understand the round flow before you start throwing chips at glowing buttons like the machine owes you money.

The basic idea

Bubble craps is an electronic version of craps. Instead of a shooter throwing dice across a live table, dice bounce inside a covered machine. You choose bets, the dice roll, and the machine pays or collects based on the total.

Most basic play starts with a come-out roll. That first roll either wins, loses, or sets a point. If a point is set, the round continues until that point repeats or a 7 rolls.

  • Come-out roll: the first roll of a new round.
  • Point: 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10 after the come-out roll.
  • Seven-out: rolling a 7 before the point repeats.
  • Bankroll: the money you are using for the session.

Round flow

1. Come-out roll A new round begins. Pass Line and Don’t Pass can resolve immediately or move into the point phase.
2. Point is set If the come-out roll is 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10, that number becomes the point.
3. Point phase Pass Line wants the point to repeat before a 7. Don’t Pass wants a 7 before the point repeats.
4. Round resets After the point is made or a 7 appears, the next roll starts a new come-out roll.

Bet meanings

What Each Bubble Craps Bet Means

Before you chase dice totals, know what your bet is actually asking for. Revolutionary concept, apparently.

Pass Line

The Pass Line is the basic “with the shooter” bet. It is usually the easiest place for beginners to start.

  • Come-out 7 or 11: wins.
  • Come-out 2, 3, or 12: loses.
  • Come-out 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10: sets the point.
  • After point: point before 7 wins; 7 before point loses.

Don’t Pass

Don’t Pass is the opposite side of the basic game. On bubble craps, no table full of humans can glare at you. The machine is emotionally unavailable.

  • Come-out 2 or 3: wins.
  • Come-out 7 or 11: loses.
  • Come-out 12: usually pushes.
  • After point: 7 before point wins; point before 7 loses.

Field

The Field is a one-roll bet. It resolves on the next roll, which makes it feel exciting and makes bankroll leaks happen at professional speed.

  • Usually wins: 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11, or 12.
  • Usually loses: 5, 6, 7, or 8.
  • Pay table warning: 2 and 12 payouts can vary by machine. This simulator pays 2:1 on 2 and 12.
  • Beginner note: fun for action, easy to overuse.

Strategy without fairy dust

Simple Bubble Craps Strategies People Use

These are risk-control approaches, not secret ways to bully dice into employment.

Flat Betting

Bet the same amount each round. This keeps results easier to understand and helps you see whether bet size is too large for the bankroll.

Pass Line Only

Stick with Pass Line while learning. It follows the main round structure and avoids the noise of side bets.

Don’t Pass Only

Use Don’t Pass if you prefer the opposite side of the point phase. It is not magic. It is just the other side of the basic bet.

Small Field Bets

Some players use tiny Field bets for action. Keep them small. Fast-resolving bets can drain a bankroll before your common sense finds its shoes.

Odds Bets, Where Offered

After a point is set, some tables and machines let you add a free odds bet that pays true odds with no house edge. It lowers the combined edge (Pass + 1x odds: 0.85%; 3-4-5x: 0.37%) but puts more money on each decision, so swings get bigger. This simulator does not include odds bets.

Beginner traps

Common Bubble Craps Mistakes

Most bad sessions are not mysterious. They are usually bet size, speed, and pretending the dice have unfinished business with you.

Betting before understanding the point

Once a point is set, the round changes. Pass Line wants the point. Don’t Pass wants the 7.

Overusing the Field

The Field resolves every roll, which makes it feel active. Fast action can also drain money faster.

Betting too large

A $100 bankroll with $25 chips is not a plan. It is a short documentary about regret.

Chasing after losses

The next roll does not owe you an apology. Increasing your bet after a loss mostly increases the size of the next problem.

Craps math basics

Bubble Craps Math: House Edge, Odds, and Variance

Bubble craps uses the same dice math as a live craps table. Two dice make 36 equally likely combinations, and 7 is the most common total (6 of 36, or 16.7%). What changes from machine to machine is the pay table and the list of bets offered, so check the machine's help screen before you play. The numbers below assume standard payouts.

House edge by bet

House edge is the average share of each bet the game keeps over the long run. A 1.41% edge means an average loss of about $1.41 for every $100 bet, not for every $100 you bring.

Place and Buy edges are per bet that wins or loses, and one-roll bets are per roll. Watch the wording on one-roll bets. "30 for 1" means 29 to 1 once your stake is counted, which raises the edge on a 2 or 12 from 13.89% to 16.67%.

Why odds bets matter

After a point is set, many tables and some machines let you add a free odds bet behind Pass Line or Don't Pass. It pays true odds, so it has no house edge. Adding odds lowers the combined edge (Pass Line alone 1.41%, with 1x odds 0.85%, with 3-4-5x odds 0.37%) because more of your money sits on a bet the house does not profit from. It also puts more money on each decision, so swings get bigger. This simulator does not include odds bets.

Variance: why short sessions swing so much

A Pass Line decision takes about 3.4 rolls on average, and each decision wins or loses about one full bet. The Field is settled on every roll. That difference matters more than it looks.

Take 100 rolls at $5 a bet:

  • Pass Line: about 30 decisions, an expected loss of about $2.09, and a typical swing (one standard deviation) of about $27.
  • Field, every roll: 100 decisions, an expected loss of about $27.78, and a typical swing of about $54.

The Field costs about 13 times more per 100 rolls because it charges 5.56% on every roll, while the Pass Line charges 1.41% about once every 3.4 rolls. In both cases the swing is much larger than the expected loss, which is why a short session can end well ahead or well behind. Over many sessions the results drift toward the house edge.

Why no betting system beats the edge

Every bet on the layout has a negative expected value. Your long-run expected loss is the house edge of each bet times the total amount you bet on it, added up. Betting systems such as Martingale (doubling after a loss), pressing after wins, or switching between Pass and Don't Pass change how much you bet and when. They do not change the edge on any single bet, so they cannot turn the total into a profit.

The dice also have no memory. Each roll is independent, so a "hot" machine, a streak of sevens, or a number that "hasn't hit in a while" tells you nothing about the next roll. You can watch a progression system run into this in the roulette strategy simulator, where every system loses about the same share of each dollar bet.

What this means at the machine

  • Pass Line or Don't Pass, plus odds where the machine offers them, keeps the edge lowest.
  • Hardways and one-roll proposition bets carry 9% to 17% edges (one-roll bets alone run 11% to 17%).
  • The Field is simple, but at 2:1 on both 2 and 12 it costs about four times as much per bet as the Pass Line, and it settles every roll.
  • Pick your bet size and a loss limit before the first roll. Our bankroll tools can help with that.

This is educational math, not a way to win. The house keeps its edge on every bet, gambling involves real financial risk, and you should play only where it is legal and only with money you can afford to lose.

FAQ

Bubble Craps FAQ

Short answers for the questions people usually have before the dice start pretending to be entertainment.

What is bubble craps?

Bubble craps is an electronic craps game where dice bounce inside a covered machine instead of being thrown at a live table.

Is bubble craps the same as regular craps?

The core bets are similar, but the machine interface, pace, minimum bets, and available side bets can differ from a live craps table.

What is the come-out roll?

The come-out roll is the first roll of a new round. It can win, lose, or set a point for Pass Line and Don’t Pass bets.

What does the point mean?

The point is 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10 after the come-out roll. Pass Line wants that number again before 7. Don’t Pass wants 7 first.

What is the Field bet?

The Field is a one-roll bet. It wins or loses on the next dice total and does not carry into the point phase.

Can strategy remove the house edge in bubble craps?

You can make better risk decisions, but you cannot strategy your way out of the house edge. Control bet size, avoid bad habits, and know when to stop.
